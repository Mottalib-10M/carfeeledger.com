                         Car Dealer Document Fee Caps

                                       By: Heather Poole, Principal Analyst
                                           March 8, 2023| 2023-R-0092


Issue
Provide the maximum dealer document fees in states that cap them and the services included
under the fees.

Summary
Dealer document fees (also called documentary fees, “doc” fees, processing fees, conveyance fees,
handling fees, and administrative fees) are fees car dealers may charge for processing documents
and providing other services related to a vehicle sale.

In Connecticut, these fees are called dealer conveyance fees. By law, a conveyance fee is a fee car
dealers may charge to “recover reasonable costs for processing all documentation and performing
services related to the closing of a sale, including, but not limited to, the registration and transfer of
ownership of the motor vehicle which is the subject of the sale” (CGS § 14-62(a)). Connecticut law
does not cap the conveyance fee or define “reasonable costs,” but it sets requirements for the
notice and information that a dealer must provide consumers about the fee (see OLR Report 2018-
R-0063 for more information on these requirements). A 2016 study by the Program Review and
Investigations Committee found that the average dealer conveyance fee in Connecticut was $405
for new car dealers and $131 for used car dealers.

At least 15 states cap dealer document fees. The caps currently range from $70 (or $85) in
California to $565.38 in Missouri. Each state defines these fees differently, but like Connecticut’s,
most definitions are generally broad enough to encompass fees related to (1) processing
registration, titling, lien, financing, and tax documentation; (2) information privacy and data security
compliance; (3) record retention and document storage requirements; and (4) other administrative
