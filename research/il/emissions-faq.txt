1.   What changes are being made to the Vehicle Emission Test Program?

    There are four new types of options for motorists. No appointment is needed for any of
    the four options.

    1.  The addition of an Air Team test site in Chicago at 6959 West Forest Preserve Drive. It
         is equipped with 10 testing positions and is anticipated to accommodate up to 300,000
         cars per year.

    2.  The addition of five (5) inspection and repair test sites around Chicago.
             •      Car-X 7424 S. Stoney Island Avenue, Chicago
             •      Car-X 2737 N. Central Ave., Chicago
             •      Car-X 2509 N. Ashland Ave., Chicago
             •      Car-X 5418 S. Pulaski Road, Chicago
             •   Pete’s Auto Service 7800 S. Kedzie Ave., Chicago

    3.  The addition of two Mobile Testing Units (MTUs), one specifically to be stationed in
         Chicago. The MTUs will be at various locations throughout Chicago and the
         Chicagoland area. A calendar of locations will be on the Illinois Air Team website.

    4.  The addition of Self-Service Kiosks. The first kiosk will be available at Air Team test
         station, 6959 West Forest Preserve Drive, Chicago. Additional kiosks are planned for
         other locations in the city and will be listed on the Air Team website station locator
         when available.

2.   When will the new testing sites be available to motorists?

    The Forest Preserve station will re-open March 17th; the MTUs will become operational at
    the same time. A kiosk at the Forest Preserve location will be available shortly after, with
    additional kiosk deployments in the near future.

3.   What are the hours of operation for the new sites?

    The Forest Preserve location is available Monday through Friday 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM and
    Saturdays 7:30 AM to 1:00 PM.

    The inspection and maintenance test sites are available Monday through Friday 8:00 AM
    to 6:00 PM and Saturdays 7:30 AM to 1:00 PM.

    The Forest Preserve Kiosk will be available 24-hours a day, 7 days a week.
    The MTUs will operate on variable schedules.               Please see the calendar on the Air Team
    website for more details.

4.  Can anyone use the new facilities?

    Yes, anyone that has been notified that their vehicle needs testing may use the sites.

5.  Is there a cost to the new options?

    No.   Testing is free, whether at an existing high-capacity test station or at one of the new
    options (inspection and maintenance locations, self-service kiosk, MTU).

6.  Do I need an appointment?

    No, none of the test services require an appointment.

7.  How can I find the closest test station to me?

    The Air Team website has an online Station Locator that provides distance, wait time and
    a map link for all of the stations in the program.

8.  Can I find out what the wait is prior to driving to one of the facilities?

    Yes. The Air Team website includes wait time information for the Air Team test stations
    and the inspection and maintenance test stations.

9.  How will I know where the MTUs are located on any given day?

    The Air Team website includes a calendar of locations for the MTUs.

10. How do you use a self-service kiosk, and is it difficult?

    to the user.    Also, there will be a video on the Air Team website that walk users through
    the process.

14. Are kiosks and MTUs       viable options for motorists?

    Kiosks are regularly used in similar I/M programs in Ohio, Maryland, and Wisconsin.
    Mobile testing vans have been successfully used in I/M programs in Tennessee and
    Massachusetts.      The goal of the program is to make testing as convenient as possible for
    motorists. By providing multiple options, motorists can hopefully find a test option
    suitable and convenient for their varied individual needs.

15. Why not build more centralized stations?

    New technology provides more convenient options for motorists to obtain a vehicle
    emissions testing. Kiosks and mobile testing units provide flexible new opportunities that
    have proven popular in other States. The overall cost, permitting constraints, land
    availability, and time it takes to build centralized facilities restricts options to build new
    test facilities and does not provide the locational or hourly flexibility offered by MTUs and
    self-service kiosks.

17. How do I know if my car requires testing?

    A test notice is mailed up to four (4) months prior to your vehicle’s license plate expiration
    reminding you to take your vehicle in for an emissions test. The Illinois EPA urges
    motorists to have their vehicles tested several weeks prior to license plate expiration to
    allow sufficient time to complete the process. If a vehicle fails the emissions test, it may
    be necessary to have emissions-related repairs performed to the vehicle, and the vehicle
    must pass its retest prior to the time its vehicle registration expires. To determine whether
    your vehicle is due for an emissions test, use the Vehicle Eligibility Checktool.
