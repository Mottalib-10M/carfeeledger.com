Budget
Vehicle Registration Fee
ACHD has long prioritized responsible budgeting and the efficient use of taxpayer dollars. As Ada County continues to grow, we’ve worked to keep pace by planning, building, and maintaining a transportation system that meets the needs of our community - while remaining thoughtful and fiscally responsible in how we deliver those services. However, rising costs are placing increasing pressure on the resources that support our core operations, including maintaining safe and reliable roads for the public.

Official Ballot Language
STATEMENT OF PURPOSE
In 1990, Ada County voters approved a vehicle registration fee to fund transportation improvements throughout the county. In 2008, Ada County voters renewed the vehicle registration fee. The fee has not increased since that time. Since 1990, revenue from the fee has supported road and bridge repair, congestion relief, and safe routes to school for Ada County residents.
The current vehicle registration fee is authorized through December 31, 2028. This measure would reauthorize the existing fee through December 31, 2046 at its current rates, with no increase. Revenue generated by the fee would continue to be used for transportation improvements within the jurisdiction of the Ada County Highway District.
WHAT THIS MEASURE DOES
This measure continues the existing vehicle registration fee at its current rates, set in 2008. No fee amount is increased. The approximately $14 million generated annually will be used for construction and maintenance projects under the jurisdiction of the Ada County Highway District, including road and bridge repair, traffic congestion relief, and safe routes to school.
CURRENT FEES:
For each automobile, pickup truck, or motor vehicle with a maximum gross weight not exceeding 8,000 pounds, designed to carry passengers and not for hire:
• Vehicles over seven years old: $24
• Vehicles between three and seven years old: $36
• Vehicles one to two years old: $40
• Motor vehicles primarily used for hire and each school bus: $16.00
• Motorcycles and terrain vehicles: $8.00
YES (APPROVE) Extend fee to 2046 with no increase.
NO (DISAPPROVE) Let fee expire in 2028.

Why This Matters
Vehicle Registration Fees play a significant role in keeping Ada County moving. They provide ACHD with a reliable, locally controlled funding source that goes directly back into our community - supporting transportation projects that reduce congestion, improve safety, and enhance quality of life.
These funds help deliver meaningful, neighborhood-level improvements, from safer intersections and smoother roadways to better access for people walking, biking, and rolling to road and bridge repairs. They also support critical investments in Safe Routes to School, helping students and families travel to and from school more safely.
By investing locally, Vehicle Registration Fees allow ACHD to respond to the needs of our growing community, maintain our infrastructure, and build a transportation system that works for everyone - today and into the future.
Is this a tax increase?
No. This is a continuation of the existing fee at the current rates. Fees are charged when a vehicle is registered. This ballot measure has no impact on property, gas, or sales taxes. 
Who pays the Vehicle Registration Fee?
Most vehicles registered in Ada County are subject to the Vehicle Registration Fee. The fee is paid for each eligible vehicle at the time of registration.
Will fees be going up?
No. This measure is a continuation of the current fees, which were first established in 2008. ACHD is not proposing an increase in the fees, meaning the current registration fee would remain the same. 
What will this cost me?
For most passenger vehicles, the VRF works out to between $0.46 and $0.77 per week. Please see the cost breakdown above. 
What happens if the ballot measure fails?
The current Vehicle Registration Fee sunsets at the end of 2028. If the ballot measure fails this year (2026), ACHD may choose to place another measure on the ballot during the 2028 general election. Should that measure also fail, ACHD would lose around $14 million each year in dedicated funding for congestion management and Safe Routes to School projects (such as sidewalk projects). 
Where can the money from VRF be used?
The VRF applies to all areas under ACHD’s jurisdiction, which includes Boise, Garden City, Meridian, Eagle, Star, and Kuna, along with unincorporated areas of Ada County. Funds can be used anywhere in ACHD’s right of way. 
How are VRF funds used?
The VRF funds traffic congestion reduction projects and neighborhood-level improvements under ACHD jurisdiction, including Safe Routes to School, intersection safety, sidewalks, bike lanes, and traffic calming. VRF funds can be used county-wide where ACHD has jurisdiction. 
Are these funds limited to certain types of projects?
State and federal funds are typically reserved for major arterials and highways. The VRF specifically targets local neighborhood improvements, traffic calming, and pedestrian/bicycle safety that wouldn't otherwise be funded.
Who decides which projects get VRF funding?
All projects are approved by the Ada County Highway District Board of Commissioners. Commissioners also approve the agency’s annual budget. 
How are projects selected?
ACHD uses a data-driven project selection process, along with additional programming directives and guidelines, which include evaluating safety needs and overall feasibility, in-depth scoping and traffic analysis, partner agency priority and community input. Projects are included in ACHD's publicly adopted budget, as well as its Five Year Plan.   
When will I be able to vote? 
Eligible Ada County residents will have a chance to vote on the ballot measure during the general election on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. 
Who can vote on the ballot measure?
Eligible voters residing in Ada County will have a chance to vote on the ballot measure during the general election on Tuesday, November 3, 2026. For information on voting, such as finding your registration status, polling place, and other election information, visit Ada County Elections at: 
https://adacounty.id.gov/elections/
What other funding sources does ACHD use for roadway projects?
ACHD has several funding sources. For example, development impact fees are collected from new development and used to fund eligible roadway expansion projects to accommodate new development. ACHD also typically receives funding through the state, as well as federal grants. ACHD also receives money from the state’s fuel tax. While these are all important funding sources, they may be subject to restrictions based on funding type. ACHD also receives property and sales taxes to support operations and maintenance. The Vehicle Registration Fee is one of several funding sources that support transportation improvements within ACHD's jurisdiction. 
How do I pay the Vehicle Registration Fee?
The Vehicle Registration Fee is paid when a vehicle is registered. For more information on vehicle registration, please visit: 
https://adacounty.id.gov/assessor/dmv-registrations-renewals-titles/vehicle-registration-renewals/
What does a “Yes” vote mean? 
A "yes" vote means a voter supports allowing the Ada County Highway District to continue collecting the existing Vehicle Registration Fee for transportation safety and mobility improvements until 2046. Fees will not increase. 
What does a “No” vote mean? 
A "no" vote means the existing Vehicle Registration Fee would continue only through the end of 2028, when it is scheduled to sunset. 
Why is ACHD running this ballot measure now? 
This type of ballot measure can only appear on the ballot during general elections held in even-numbered years. Placing the measure on the 2026 ballot gives voters an opportunity to decide before the current fee expires at the end of 2028. 
What kind of oversight or public input is there on projects?
The elected Ada County Highway District Board of Commissioners oversees all VRF expenditures. The board holds regular public meetings and all spending is subject to public record and independent audit.
Can Vehicle Registration Fees be used for things other than transportation?
No. Vehicle Registration Fee revenue may only be used for transportation purposes consistent with the language approved by voters in the ballot measure. 
What vote is needed in order for the ballot measure to pass? 
The ballot measure needs a simple majority voting in favor in order to pass.
