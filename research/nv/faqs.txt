 To upgrade, you must visit a DMV office in person to present
 proof of identity and residency. This is a one-time
 requirement.
 Do I have one? Check for a gold star in
 the upper right corner of your card.
 Is it required? You may choose to keep
 a standard ID, but it won't be valid for boarding
 domestic flights.
 What do I need? Take the quiz at

 You have the choice of retaining a standard Nevada

 What does "NOT VALID FOR FEDERAL OFFICIAL USE" or "NOT FOR
 FEDERAL PURPOSES" mean?
 This label indicates your card is a Standard ID rather than
 a REAL ID. It means the holder chose a non-compliant card or
 did not provide the documents required by the federal REAL
 ID Act.
 Restrictions: These cards cannot be
 used to board domestic flights or enter secure federal
 facilities, like military bases or nuclear plants.
 Privacy: This label does not indicate
 your citizenship or immigration status; people choose
 standard IDs for many personal or religious reasons.
 More Info: See the
 REAL
 ID Act in Nevada 
 for full details.

 Can I keep my out-of-state license?
 No. Nevada law requires you to surrender your out-of-state
 license or ID to get a Nevada one.
 The Rule: You may only hold one valid
 credential at a time under the National Driver License
 Agreement.
 Exception: If you live in Nevada
 part-time and are not employed here, you may qualify for
 a Seasonal Resident ID, which allows you to keep your
 out-of-state driver’s license.
 See
 ID
 Cards 
 for seasonal resident qualifications
 Can I drive in Nevada with an out-of-state or foreign license?
 Yes. Nevada honors valid licenses and
 instruction permits from other U.S. states and foreign
 countries.
 Foreign Visitors: An International
 Driver License (IDL) is not required, but it is
 recommended as a translation. An IDL is not valid on its
 own; it must be accompanied by your government-issued
 license.
 Teen Drivers: If you are under 18, you
 must follow all
 Nevada
 Teen Driving 
 restrictions while driving here.
 New Residents: You must switch to a
 Nevada license within 30 days of establishing residency.

 My vehicle and/or license plates were stolen. What do I do?
 File a Police Report: Contact local law
 enforcement immediately to report the theft.
 Replace Stolen Plates: You must visit a
 DMV office in person to get a new plate number.
 Bring a completed
 VP 202
 Affidavit .
 Note: Stolen plate numbers
 cannot be duplicated; you will receive a new
 design or number.
 Stolen Vehicle: Notify the DMV to
 cancel your registration. This protects you from
 liability if the vehicle is involved in future
 incidents.
 Lost Decals or Registration: If your
 documents were simply misplaced, not stolen, you can get
 a duplicate instantly at a
 Kiosk 
 or through
 MyDMV .
 Address Check: Ensure your address
 is current. If you have moved, you must file a
 Change
 of Address 
 within 30 days.

 My registration renewal is due but my car is under repair and I
 can't get a smog check.
 Nevada does not offer registration extensions. If your car
 is being repaired, follow these steps:
 Let it Expire: Allow the registration
 to lapse. When repairs are done, get a Movement Permit
 via
 MyDMV 
 to drive to an emissions station.
 Late Fee Waiver: You can avoid late
 fees if the vehicle was not driven on public streets.
 You must renew via
 MyDMV 
 or in person and complete an Affidavit of Non-Operation.
 Note: Fees cannot be waived at
 kiosks or emissions stations. The system will
 automatically calculate late fees.
 Renewal Option: You may then renew
 online, at a kiosk or at an emissions station.
 Battery Reset: If the battery was
 disconnected, drive the car for several days before the
 smog test so the computer system can reset.
 Insurance Warning: Do not cancel your
 insurance while the plates are active. If the car will
 be down for a long time,
 Surrender
 Your Plates 
 and cancel the registration to avoid fines.
 My renewal is due but my car is out of state.
 You must renew by mail or fax if your vehicle requires a
 smog check. If not, you may renew online but the renewal is
 sent to the address on record. We cannot send it to a
 temporary address.
 See
 Renewal
 Options 
 for details.
 My renewal is due but I'll be moving out of Nevada. Is there any
 temporary registration?
 No. Nevada does not offer partial-year
 registrations or refunds. You must either register the
 vehicle for a full year or use a movement permit to drive
 legally until you leave.
 In-State Movement Permit: If you are
 still in Nevada, you can get a 10-day permit for $1
 through your
 MyDMV 
 account or at a DMV office. These are for travel within
 Nevada only.
 “Drive-Away” Permit: If you are leaving
 the state immediately, you can purchase a special permit
 at a DMV office that allows you to drive the vehicle to
 your new destination.
 Former Residents: Once you have moved
 and registered your vehicle in your new state, you
 should
 Surrender
 your Nevada Plates 
 and cancel your Nevada registration online to avoid any
 insurance fines or issues.
 Note: If your registration is
 currently active, you can simply drive to your new
 state and register there. You do not need a permit
 unless your Nevada registration has already expired.

 I didn't receive a renewal notice. What do I do?
 It is your responsibility to renew on time. Registrations
 expire on a specific date , not at the end
 of the month, and late fees are not refunded.
 Check Status: Use the
 Registration
 Status Inquiry 
 requires Plate # and last 4 of VIN to check your
 expiration date and insurance status.
 How to Renew: You don't need a notice.
 Get a smog check, if needed, and renew at a
 Kiosk 
 or via
 MyDMV 
 using your Plate and VIN.
 Address Issues: If you moved, the DMV
 does not use postal forwarding. Use a Kiosk to get your
 decal instantly, then file a
 Change
 of Address 
 online.
 Warning: If you renew online
 with an old address, your decal will be mailed
 to the wrong house.
 Timing: Notices are mailed 30 days
 before expiration. If your date is further away, it
 hasn't been sent yet.
 Note: If your registration is
 suspended due to insurance, you must reinstate it
 before you can renew. Use
 MyDMV 
 to handle both at once.
 Why isn't my online renewal working?
 If the website isn't processing your renewal, check these
 common roadblocks:
 Smog Check: If required, it must be
 completed before you try to renew online. The results
 are sent electronically to the DMV.
 Plate & VIN Accuracy: Enter your
 plate number exactly as it appears on your registration,
 no spaces or dashes. For specialty plates, ensure you
 include the correct
 Prefix
 Code .
 Renewal Window: You can typically only
 renew online starting 35 days before
 your expiration date.
 Account Holds: The system will block
 renewals for:
 Insurance Suspensions: Check
 your status via
 Registration
 Inquiry 
 Unpaid Parking Tickets: These
 must be settled with the specific city or county
 first.
 Bad Debt: Any outstanding fees
 owed to the DMV.
 Technical Issues: Ensure JavaScript and
 cookies are enabled. If the site still fails, try a
 different browser or use a
 DMV
 Now Kiosk 
 for an instant decal.
 Note: If you are still getting an
 error message, like a specific code, you may need to
 renew in person or by mail. You can also email the
 DMV with your plate number and VIN for specific
 technical help.

 If you receive a renewal or insurance notice for a vehicle

 Respond to Insurance Notices: Use the
 Insurance
 Verification Response

 Cancel Your Registration: Log into

 The Risk of Doing Nothing: You won’t

 How do I get a VIN inspection?
 You have three main options to get an out-of-state vehicle
 inspected:
 DMV Office (Free): Drive to any
 full-service DMV during business hours. Larger offices
 have a dedicated “VIN Station” lane on the side or rear
 of the building. No appointment is needed for the
 inspection itself.
 Authorized Businesses: Private
 companies like Smog Busters can perform inspections for
 a small fee. This is often faster than the DMV and
 doesn't require an appointment.
 Law Enforcement: You can have a sworn
 officer complete the
 VP 015
 Certificate .
 Note that many agencies only offer this for residents in
 rural areas or by special request.
 What to bring: The vehicle and its
 out-of-state title or registration.
 Why do you require odometer readings?
 The Nevada Legislature directed the DMV to collect odometer
 readings to collect mileage data to help legislators plan
 for future road construction funding. See
 Odometer 
 for more details.

 Can I get a refund of my registration fees?
 In most cases, no. Nevada typically issues a credit rather
 than a refund. This credit is applied to the registration of
 your next vehicle. See
 Plate
 Surrender/Refunds .
 Note: No refunds are issued to
 former residents.
 How can I find out how much my registration fees will be?
 You can get an estimate or see your exact total using these
 DMV tools:
 For a New Vehicle: Use the
 Online
 Fee Estimate .
 You’ll need the VIN and the original MSRP, the price
 when the car was brand new.
 For a Renewal: Log into your
 One-Time
 Renewal 
 or
 MyDMV 
 account. It will show your total, including any late
 fees.
 Note: You must complete a smog check
 first if one is required.
 Note: Nevada fees are based on the
 original MSRP, not what you paid for the car. The
 tax amount drops by about 10% each year until the
 vehicle is 9 years old.
 How are registration fees calculated?
 Your total is based on two main parts:
 Flat Fee: $33 for most cars and motorcycles.
 Taxes (GST): Calculated using 35% of the original MSRP,
 which then depreciates, drops, as the vehicle ages.
 Local Tax: Residents in Clark or Washoe counties pay an
 extra 1% for road projects.
 Key Rule: The tax is always based
 on the original sticker price, not what you paid for
 the car used. Use the
 Online
 Fee Estimate 
 with your VIN for an exact total.

 I just bought a vehicle. What are the registration requirements?
 You must register within 30 days of purchase.
 Online: For Vehicle Purchased from an
 Out-of-State Dealer, New Resident, Private Party or
 Gifted. See
 Rapid
 Registration .
 Nevada dealer: If you purchased a
 vehicle from a Nevada dealer, you may be able to
 register it
 Online 
 without visiting a DMV office. You can transfer existing
 plates or obtain new plates by mail. They should be
 providing you with an EDRS #. If not, please ask your
 dealership that you purchased your car from.
 Note: You must have insurance and a
 movement permit or dealer placard to drive any
 vehicle on public streets. See
 Vehicle
 Registration Requirements .
 I want to sell a vehicle to a family member or give a vehicle as
 a gift.
 Transferring a car to a family member is treated as a
 Private Party Sale.
 The Process: Sign the Title over to the
 family member. They can then register it online via
 Rapid
 Registration 
 (DriveNV).
 Fees: Credits and fees cannot be
 transferred. The new owner must pay for a full year of
 registration.
 Plates: Normally, you keep your plates
 for credit, and they get new ones.
 To let them keep your physical plates, you must
 sign a
 License Plate
 Release (SP 67) .
 Taxes: Gifts and sales to immediate
 family are exempt from sales tax.
 Insurance: The new owner must get their
 own Nevada insurance before registering.
 Note: Even if you “sell” the car
 for $1, the DMV still calculates registration fees
 based on the vehicle’s original MSRP value.
 I want to donate a vehicle to charity.
 Donating is treated like a Private Party Sale. Follow these
 steps:
 The Title: You must provide a clear,
 signed-off title. Any loans or liens must be paid off
 first.
 Remove Your Plates: Always take your
 plates off.
 If it protects you from liability, tolls,
 tickets.
 It allows you to keep your registration credit
 for your next car.
 Notify the DMV: Complete the Vehicle
 Resale Notification online via
 MyDMV 
 within 5 days to report the transfer.
 Deadlines: You have 60 days, 30 for
 specialty plates, to either use the plates on another
 car or surrender them.
 Receipts: Keep all documents from the
 charity for your tax records.
 Insurance: Do not cancel your
 Nevada insurance until after you have removed the
 plates and notified the DMV. Canceling early can
 trigger automatic fines.
 I recently paid off a loan or other lien or bought out a lease.
 What do I need to do?
 Loan Payoffs:
 Electronic Titles: The bank notifies
 the DMV, and a clear title is mailed to you
 automatically.
 Paper Titles: The bank signs the
 “Release of Lien” and mails it to you. You should then
 visit the DMV to apply for a fresh “clear” title.
 Lease Buyouts:
 Treated as a vehicle sale. You must pay sales tax on the
 buyout price.
 Update your registration at the DMV to show you as the
 owner, approx. $5 fee.
 The dealer/leasing company usually handles the title
 transfer to you.
 Important: Ensure your lender has
 your correct mailing address before your final
 payment so your title doesn’t get lost in the mail.

 I want to get a personalized or specialty license plate.
 You can upgrade your plates even if your registration isn't
 due.
 How to Choose: Browse styles and check
 personalized text availability using the
 Online Plate
 Search .
 How to Order: 
 Online/Mail: Best if your car
 is already registered. The DMV will mail a
 postcard when your plates are ready for pickup,
 usually 4–6 weeks.
 In-Person: Best if you are
 currently registering or renewing. You'll get a
 movement permit, and the plates will be mailed
 to your home.
 Fees: 
 Swap Fee: $5, plus
 plate-specific costs, if you aren't renewing
 your registration.
 Plate Costs: Range from $3.50,
 standard, up to $96, specialty/personalized.
 Annual Fee: Most specialty
 plates have an extra yearly fee, $20–$30, added
 to your renewal.
 The Swap: You must visit a DMV office
 to pick up new plates. Bring your old plates to
 surrender them.
 Tip: Use
 Rapid
 Registration 
 to select a specialty background online during your
 very first registration of a new vehicle.
 I want to transfer my plates from my old vehicle to my new
 vehicle, what do I do?
 You can move your existing plates to a new car to apply
 registration credits, unused fees, toward your new total.
 How to do it:
 Online: Select the “Transfer”
 option during registration.
 In-Person: Inform the DMV
 technician you are transferring plates.
 Cost: $5 transfer fee plus standard
 registration costs.
 The "Don't" Rule: Do not cancel your
 old registration online before starting the new one, or
 you won't be able to transfer the plates digitally.
 Giving Plates to Others: To let someone
 else use your plates, sign a License Plate Release (SP
 67).
 Note: Only the plates transfer; your
 money/credits do not.
 Take your plates with you: If you
 leave them on a car you sold or traded in, you lose
 your fee credit and remain liable for the vehicle.
 How do I get handicapped plates or placards?
 To obtain disabled parking privileges, you must have a
 qualifying medical condition certified by a healthcare
 professional.
 The Form: Download and print
 Application
 SP-27 .
 Medical Certification: A licensed
 Physician, APRN, PA, or Physical/Occupational Therapist
 must complete the medical section. See
 Disabled
 Parking .
 Does Nevada honor handicapped placards or license plates from
 other states or counties?
 Yes, Nevada honors valid disabled placards
 and plates from other states or countries.
 Requirement: They must be issued by a
 government agency; private permits are invalid.
 Usage: The permit holder must be
 present in the vehicle.
 Fees: A placard does not exempt you
 from parking meter or garage fees.
 New Residents: If you move to Nevada,
 you must apply for a Nevada-specific permit.
 Important: Ensure your placard is
 clearly visible. The fine for illegal parking in a
 disabled space starts at $250. See
 Disabled
 Parking
